Canada's third-largest aerospace cluster.

And the largest in Western Canada.

  • 1,500StandardAero people across eight Winnipeg facilities.
  • 43Member and associate member profiles, from global primes to specialist shops, colleges, agencies and not-for-profits.

StandardAero overhauls engines for airlines and the RCAF here, with a 70,000 sq ft expansion that opened in September 2026. Magellan machines fighter engine components and builds Black Brant rockets. GE runs an engine research and test centre in Winnipeg, and SkyAlyne trains the RCAF's future aircrew from Winnipeg and Southport.

Aerospace in Manitoba →

The RCAF's operations are run from Winnipeg.

So is the Canadian NORAD Region.

1 Canadian Air Division and the Canadian NORAD Region are headquartered at CFB Winnipeg, 17 Wing. With CFB Shilo and the Southport Aerospace Centre, Manitoba is where Canada's air defence is commanded and its aircrew trained, and where the suppliers that keep it flying already work.

$19.5Min federal defence investment in Winnipeg, April 2026.

Three Winnipeg projects. $8M for Magellan's advanced machining centre, making F404 and F414 engine parts for the Super Hornet, the Growler and the Gripen. $8M for StandardAero to expand engine work for the RCAF's CC-130H, CC-130J and CP-140, and the P-8A Poseidon once it is in service. $3.5M for Win-Shield Devices. More than 150 jobs created and 100 kept.

Hoar frost crystals. Stand-in photo.

Jet engines are proven in Manitoba's cold.

GLACIER, in Thompson, is run by Rolls-Royce and Pratt & Whitney with the National Research Council. It tests engines of up to 150,000 lbf thrust in natural icing weather. MDs AeroTest, which built it with the NRC, and EnviroTREC, its not-for-profit partner, are both members.

One door into Manitoba's defence supply chain.

The Manitoba-Canada Defence Alliance launched on September 8, 2026, joining government, industry, post-secondary, the Canadian Armed Forces and Indigenous partners, with a dedicated defence lead based at the association. Alongside it, the Manitoba Defence and Dual-Use Innovation Bridge, over $3.8M across two years, prepares 100 Manitoba SMEs for defence work: cyber, controlled goods, dual-use products and procurement.
