The industry today

Manitoba already builds and services the hardware Canada and its allies fly.

$19.5M

Federal defence investment in three Winnipeg projects, including Magellan’s machining for F404 and F414 fighter engines and StandardAero’s engine work for the RCAF’s CC-130 and CP-140.

Government of Canada, April 2026

By the association’s own 2023 count, Manitoba aerospace sold more than $2B in products and services a year, employed more than 5,000 people directly and served customers on six continents. In September 2026, StandardAero opened a 70,000 sq ft expansion in Winnipeg.

CC-177 Globemaster III, night ramp

Defence starts with one call.

The Manitoba-Canada Defence Alliance funds a dedicated defence lead at the association. Primes, government buyers and allied delegations get one door into the cluster.

Talk to the defence lead

  • Manitoba-Canada Defence Alliance

    Industry, government, post-secondary and the Canadian Armed Forces, launched September 8, 2026.

  • Defence and Dual-Use Innovation Bridge

    Over $3.8M across two years to prepare 100 Manitoba SMEs for defence supply chains: cyber, controlled goods, dual-use and procurement. With RRC Polytech and Canadian Manufacturers & Exporters.

  • Procurement Pathways

    Federal procurement workshop and B2B sessions with DND, Public Services and Procurement Canada, the Controlled Goods Program, Boeing Defense, Space and Security and Airbus Helicopters Canada. Third year in 2026.

Sectors

1 Canadian Air Division and the Canadian NORAD Region are headquartered at CFB Winnipeg. 17 Wing, CFB Shilo and the Southport Aerospace Centre are here too.

Defence in Manitoba

Canada’s largest aerospace composite plant at Boeing Winnipeg, plus engine overhaul, precision machining and cold-weather engine testing.

Aerospace in Manitoba

Black Brant sounding rockets are built in Winnipeg by Magellan Aerospace, along with satellite buses and propulsion.

Space in Manitoba

Built for the cold.

At GLACIER in Thompson, Rolls-Royce and Pratt & Whitney work with the National Research Council to test jet engines up to 150,000 lbf of thrust in natural icing. GE runs its own research and test centre in Winnipeg.
